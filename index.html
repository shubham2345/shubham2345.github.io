About

I am a first-year Ph.D. student in Computer Science at UC Santa Cruz, advised by Dr. Ian Lane. My research is on LLM post-training, factuality, and the safety-focused evaluation of language models and agents — where capable models quietly fail, and how to measure it well enough to fix it.

That work currently spans agent benchmarks, reward hacking in agent evaluation infrastructure, and how supportive language models shape a user's reliance on them. Before returning to research I spent six years building production ML at Nokia Bell Labs, Adani Group, and BlackRock, at 100M+ user scale.

Research

Four threads, and the projects that sit under each.

Agent Evaluation & Benchmarks

Benchmarks fail quietly: an agent can raise its score by exploiting the reward-relevant trajectory instead of solving the task, and a benchmark that never asks the hard question cannot report that it was missed. I build evaluation infrastructure that makes those failures measurable — FAULTLOC for fault localization from crash to cause, RCABench for multi-hop causal reasoning over large codebases, and reward-hacking adjudication for BenchShield.

Long-Horizon Web Agents

Web agents re-ingest the full DOM and accessibility tree at every action step, so context degrades long before the task ends. I argue this coupling of observation to action is an architectural mistake, and propose Signal-Driven Observation: re-read only when a lightweight signal fires. Related work on human interaction trajectories for WorkArena feeds the same question from the data side.

Human-Centered Alignment

Evaluations of supportive models ask whether a response is safe, empathetic, or helpful. RELATE asks a different question: where does the model point the user for support next — back to itself, or outward to the people in their life? Measured at the sentence level across multi-turn dialogue.

Post-Training, Unlearning & Factuality

Causal, Why-type knowledge is nearly absent from standard unlearning benchmarks, which lets a method look good in aggregate while failing exactly where it matters. 5WBench and MAAT address that gap; alongside it I am investigating long-tail factual recall in LLMs through reasoning and post-training, and skill optimization without task supervision in SkillOptimizer.

LLM Agents

Long-horizon web agents re-ingest tens of thousands of DOM tokens at every action step, eroding reasoning before the task completes. Decoupling observation from action — re-reading only when a URL change, a newly visible element, or an action failure fires — makes observation compression an architectural decision rather than a prompting one.
